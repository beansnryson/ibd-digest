Health-related quality of life after diagnosis of ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease: 3-year trajectories and factors associated with clinically meaningful improvement (the IBSEN III cohort)

Olsen BC, Hagen M, Opheim R, Kristensen VA, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseUlcerative ColitisOriginal Research
Show abstract

BACKGROUND: Evidence for the clinical importance of changes in health-related quality of life (HRQoL) over time in unselected patients with ulcerative colitis (UC) and Crohn's disease (CD) is limited. We aimed to describe HRQoL trajectories, determine clinically meaningful HRQoL improvement, and identify factors associated with such improvement during the first 3 years after diagnosis.

METHODS: Adults with UC and CD from a population-based inception cohort completed the Inflammatory Bowel Disease Questionnaire (IBDQ) and the 36-Item Short Form Survey (SF-36) at inclusion and at the 1-year and 3-year follow-ups. HRQoL trajectories were estimated using linear mixed-effects models. Clinically meaningful improvement was defined using established minimal clinically important difference (MCID) thresholds (IBDQ ≥16; SF-36 physical and mental component summary scores ≥3). Associations between prespecified variables and MCID achievement at follow-up were assessed using multivariable logistic regression.

RESULTS: Among 1016 patients (UC: 68%; CD: 32%), IBDQ and SF-36 physical and mental component summary scores improved at both follow-ups, and approximately half of the patients achieved the corresponding MCID thresholds. HRQoL scores remained below general Norwegian population norms, and trajectories were similar for UC and CD. Symptomatic remission was consistently associated with higher odds of clinically meaningful HRQoL improvement, whereas depressive symptoms, substantial fatigue, and irritable bowel-like symptoms were associated with lower odds. Endoscopic remission, biologic therapy, and bowel surgery during the first year were not significantly associated with clinically meaningful improvement.

CONCLUSIONS: Clinically meaningful HRQoL improvement was achieved by about half of the patients and was consistently associated with lower symptom and psychosocial burden.

Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol Oct 13 2026

Early-life animal exposure was not associated with risk of inflammatory bowel disease: a nationwide birth cohort study

Balmer C, Agrawal M, Hansen AV, Torres J, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseUlcerative ColitisOriginal Research
Show abstract

BACKGROUND AND AIMS: The incidence of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) continues to rise globally. Early-life environmental exposures may influence immune development, but evidence regarding animal exposure remains inconsistent. We examined whether early-life exposure to pets or farm animals was associated with offspring risk of IBD in a prospective birth cohort.

METHODS: In this nationwide cohort study based on the Danish National Birth Cohort, prenatal offspring exposure to pets or farm animals, based on maternal reports, was used as the primary indicator of early-life animal exposure. Early-childhood exposure from birth to 18 months was examined in exposure-window analyses. Incident IBD, Crohn's disease (CD), and ulcerative colitis (UC) were identified through national registries. Associations were estimated using Cox proportional hazards models.

RESULTS: Among 90,306 offspring, 46% were prenatally exposed to pets and 7% to farm animals. During median follow-up of 24 years, 615 offspring developed IBD. Pet exposure was not associated with IBD risk (aHR 1.05, 95% CI 0.89-1.24), nor was farm animal exposure (aHR 1.00, 95% CI 0.73-1.37). Findings were consistent across specific animal categories and in analyses of CD and UC separately. In exposure-window analyses restricted to 63,958 offspring with maternal reports of both pre- and postnatal exposure, no associations were observed among offspring exposed only during pregnancy (aHR 1.10, 95% CI 0.75-1.63) or only during early childhood (aHR 0.73, 95% CI 0.46-1.15).

CONCLUSIONS: Early-life exposure to pets or farm animals was not associated with offspring risk of IBD. These findings suggest that early-life animal exposure may not be a major determinant of disease development.

Myofibroblast Derived Transglutaminase 2 Activity is Upregulated in Stricturing Crohn's Disease and its Inhibition Exerts Anti-Fibrotic Properties

Chandra J, Czarnecki D, Mukherjee PK, West GA, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseOriginal Research
Show abstract

BACKGROUND & AIMS: Excessive accumulation of extracellular matrix (ECM) derived from human intestinal myofibroblasts (HIMF) drives the significant clinical problem of intestinal fibrosis. Fibrosis is believed to progress even in the absence of intestinal inflammation. ECM-bound transglutaminase 2 (TG2) enzyme may exert direct pro-fibrotic effects. We investigated the role of TG2 in intestinal fibrosis.

METHODS: TG2 expression and activity in human intestinal tissue and HIMF from strictured Crohn's disease (CDs), and controls were measured. ECM accumulation by HIMF in presence or absence of TG2 inhibitors was determined. Putative TG2 signaling pathways discovered in HIMF using bulk RNA sequencing (bulk-RNAseq) and single cell RNA sequencing (scRNAseq) were functionally validated. Role of TG2 in vivo was confirmed using inducible global knockout of TGM2 (encoding TG2) in two experimental fibrosis models.

RESULTS: TG activity was increased in CDs compared to controls, with up to 100% activity attributable to TG2. TG activity correlated with the degree of histopathologic tissue fibrosis. HIMF were the major source of TG2, with ≥95% of HIMF TG activity being derived from TG2. Tissue distribution of TG2 in myofibroblasts was confirmed by scRNAseq analysis. Selective inhibition of TG2 activity reduced fibronectin (FN), collagen (COL)I, III and VI accumulation by HIMF. Novel TG2 signaling pathways revealed by bulk-RNAseq, were functionally validated. Inducible TGM2 knockout prevented experimental fibrosis without affecting the degree of intestinal inflammation.

CONCLUSIONS: TG2 may be involved in inflammation-independent progression of intestinal fibrosis.

B cells regulate T cell transfer colitis by partially reconstituting luminal IgA and modulating the colonic microbiota

Urrete J, Kim M, Chilukuri A, Kaur P, et al.

IBDOriginal Research
Show abstract

Loss of immune tolerance to commensal microbial antigens is a central driver of inflammatory bowel disease. While regulatory T cells are well recognized in maintaining intestinal homeostasis, the contribution of B cells-particularly IgA-secreting cells-remains incompletely defined. In the T cell transfer colitis model, cotransfer of splenic B cells attenuates disease, an effect largely attributed to IL-10-producing regulatory B cells (Bregs). However, alternative B cell-dependent mechanisms have not been fully explored. Here, we investigated whether splenic B cells regulate T cell transfer colitis through IgA-dependent mechanisms independent of Breg-associated cytokines. We show that splenic B cells from wild-type C57BL/6 (B6) mice reconstitute bone marrow and peripheral B cell compartments in RAG1⁻/⁻ recipients, undergo class switching, migrate to the colonic lamina propria as sIgA⁺ B lineage cells, and partially restore luminal secretory IgA. B cell cotransfer significantly attenuated clinical and histological colitis without increased expression of canonical Breg-associated cytokines, including interleukin-10, transforming growth factor β, and interleukin-35 subunits. In contrast, splenic B cells from IgA-deficient mice failed to attenuate colitis despite comparable peripheral B cell reconstitution. Restoration of secretory IgA was associated with increased bacterial IgA coating, reduced fecal bacterial burden, and remodeling of the colonic microbiota, including enrichment of beneficial commensal taxa. Importantly, because IgA-deficient B cells failed to attenuate colitis despite comparable peripheral B cell reconstitution, these findings support an important role for IgA in mediating the protective effect of B cell transfer. Collectively, these findings identify IgA-mediated modulation of the microbiota as an alternate mechanism by which B cells attenuate T cell transfer colitis, highlighting a Breg-independent regulatory pathway with potential relevance to inflammatory bowel disease pathogenesis.

Clinical Course and Predictors of Biologic Therapy, Hospitalisation and Surgery: A Five-Year Prospective Study in Newly Diagnosed Inflammatory Bowel Disease

Garre A, Nuñez Ortiz A, Rubio S, Diz-Lois Palomares MT, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseUlcerative ColitisOriginal Research
Show abstract

AIMS: This study aimed to describe the characteristics of newly diagnosed inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) patients and to analyse their clinical course within the first 5 years after diagnosis, including the need for surgery, hospitalisations and treatments.

METHODS: This prospective and large multicentre incident cohort registry in Spain enrolled adults diagnosed with Crohn's disease (CD), ulcerative colitis (UC) or unclassified IBD (IBD-U) in 2017 and followed them for 5 years.

RESULTS: A total of 3301 patients were enrolled: 1526 with CD, 1633 with UC and 142 with IBD-U. Their median age at diagnosis was 43 years (interquartile range 31-56) and approximately 50% were male. Within 5 years post-diagnosis, 30% received biologics or JAK inhibitors (JAKi), 34% were hospitalised at least once and 9% underwent surgery. The cumulative incidence of biologic/JAKi treatment, hospitalisations and surgeries was higher among CD patients. Use of biologics/JAKi correlated with a more aggressive CD phenotype, greater UC extent and younger age at diagnosis. Biologics/JAKi treatment was associated with lower hospitalisation risk in both CD (Hazard ratio [HR] = 0.4, 95% confidence interval [CI] = 0.2-0.6) and UC patients (HR = 0.5, 95% CI = 0.2-0.9). However, it was not associated with the risk of abdominal surgery in CD but was linked to an increased risk of surgery in UC (HR = 11.9, 95% CI = 4.6-30.4).

CONCLUSIONS: This large multicentre cohort demonstrated a higher use of biologics/JAKi during the first 5 years after IBD diagnosis than previously reported. Treatment with biologics/JAKi was associated with reduced risk of hospitalisation. However, under current clinical practice patterns, this therapeutic approach did not translate into lower risk of abdominal surgery.

Tofacitinib 20 vs 30 mg in acute severe ulcerative colitis: a double-blind randomized controlled trial

Singh A, Garg A, Bhardwaj A, Singh D, et al.

Ulcerative ColitisRCTAnti-TNFJAK inhibitor
Show abstract

BACKGROUND & AIMS: Tofacitinib has emerged as a promising therapy for acute severe ulcerative colitis (ASUC) due to its rapid onset of action. We compared the efficacy and safety of tofacitinib 20 mg/day vs 30 mg/day, both administered with intravenous corticosteroids (IVCS), in patients with ASUC.

METHODS: This was a double-blind, pilot, randomized controlled trial. Adult patients with ASUC were randomized to receive either tofacitinib 20 mg/day (Group 1) or 30 mg/day (Group 2) along with IVCS (hydrocortisone, 100 mg IV every 6 hours). The primary endpoint was clinical response by day 7. The secondary outcome was the cumulative probability of medical rescue therapy or post discharge escalation of therapy or colectomy within 90 days.

RESULTS: Seventy-two patients were randomized, with 36 allocated to each treatment group. At day 7, clinical response was achieved in 33/36 (91.7%) patients in Group 1 and 31/36 (86.1%) in Group 2(RR 1.06, 95% CI 0.90-1.25; P = .46). The absolute difference in response rates was 5.6% (95% CI -9.98% to 21.34%). Rescue therapy was required in 3 patients in Group 1 (all received infliximab) and 5 patients in Group 2 (2 underwent colectomy and 3 received infliximab). Event-free survival at 90 days, defined as the absence of rescue therapy, treatment escalation, or colectomy, was similar between groups (83.3% vs 80.2%; log-rank P = .74). Adverse events were predominantly mild, with no reported venous thromboembolism or major adverse cardiovascular events.

CONCLUSIONS: In this exploratory pilot trial, no statistically significant differences were observed with tofacitinib 30 mg/day compared with 20 mg/day when administered with IVCS.

CLINICAL TRIAL REGISTRATION NUMBER: Clinical Trials Registry-India: CTRI/2024/10/075098.

Etrasimod reduces circulating and mucosal inflammatory lymphocyte populations and alters innate immune and non-immune cell signatures in intestinal mucosa: ELEVATE UC phase 3 clinical data

Siegmund B, Crosby CM, Ryan RD, Smith J, et al.

Ulcerative ColitisRCTS1P modulator
Show abstract

BACKGROUND AND AIMS: Innate and adaptive immune cell dysregulation contributes to ulcerative colitis pathogenesis. The sphingosine 1-phosphate receptor modulator etrasimod showed efficacy in ulcerative colitis treatment in Phase 3 ELEVATE UC studies. Changes in circulating immune cells and mucosal cell types were characterized in etrasimod-treated patients with ulcerative colitis.

METHODS: Blood (etrasimod n = 525, placebo n = 257 patients) and mucosal biopsies (etrasimod n = 411, placebo n = 200 patients) from patients in the ELEVATE UC trials were used to assess cellular and molecular changes for etrasimod versus placebo in clinical remitters and non-remitters using immunophenotyping, transcriptomic and proteomic techniques.

RESULTS: Selective and partial reduction in circulating T- and B-cell subsets was observed for etrasimod versus placebo (Week 12), with greater effects on CD4+ T cells, including Treg and Th17 cells, than CD8+ T cells, and on naive T cells and T central memory cells than T effector memory cells, without difference between remitters and non-remitters. No significant effects on monocytes nor NK cell numbers were observed. Mucosal biopsies showed corresponding reductions in T- and B-cell subsets, myeloid cells and neutrophils accompanied by downregulation of interleukin-17 and interleukin-8 signaling in both tissue and blood, most prominently in remitters, where non-immune cell changes were consistent with tissue repair.

CONCLUSIONS: Etrasimod treatment of patients with ulcerative colitis drives T- and B-lymphocyte subset reductions in blood and intestinal mucosa, neutrophil reductions in tissue and mucosal downregulation of inflammatory pathways associated with ulcerative colitis pathogenesis.

CLINICAL TRIALS REGISTRATION NUMBER: ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT03945188; NCT03996369).

Challenges and opportunities facing inflammatory bowel disease in Sub-Saharan Africa: towards a contextually relevant research and practice agenda

Cooper S, Watermeyer G, Levin D, Mhlaba Z, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseUlcerative ColitisOriginal Research
Show abstract

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), comprising Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis, has traditionally been considered a disease of Western, industrialized countries. Over the past few decades, however, many Sub-Saharan African (SSA) countries have experienced significant increases in cases. Yet, IBD remains highly neglected in SSA with most research conducted in high-income settings and treatment guidelines geared towards these settings. An emerging body of research on the sub-continent is highlighting several unique realities, dilemmas, and priorities facing IBD, and the need for evidence-based approaches that are more locally relevant and contextually tailored. In this viewpoint article we synthesize the challenges facing IBD in SSA and propose a research and practice agenda for addressing these, building on several promising initiatives already under way. Ultimately, we make a call to the international IBD community, researchers, funders, and policymakers to recognize the importance of IBD in SSA and join efforts to remedy global disparities in the quality, accessibility, and equity of IBD care.

Plasma lipopolysaccharide-binding protein (LBP), a proxy for intestinal permeability, relates to clinicopathological and immunological disease activity in inflammatory bowel disease patients at diagnosis and during disease course

Barendregt DMH, Tuk B, Hulleman-van Haaften DH, Camman AE, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseUlcerative ColitisOriginal Research
Show abstract

BACKGROUND AND AIMS: Intestinal barrier healing is the treatment goal in ulcerative colitis (UC) and Crohn's disease (CD). Despite this, systematic analyses of the relationship between intestinal permeability and clinicopathological and immunological disease activity at diagnosis and during follow-up are lacking. Therefore, we assessed this relationship using plasma lipopolysaccharide-binding protein (LBP)-an indirect biomarker of systemic exposure to bacterial endotoxins-as a proxy for intestinal permeability, in a uniquely well-characterized pediatric inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) cohort.

METHODS: LBP was measured in 1083 longitudinal plasma samples of newly diagnosed pediatric UC (n = 61), CD (n = 115) (PIBD-SETQuality and TISKids prospective cohorts; median follow-up 628 days), and controls (IBD-negative, n = 33; healthy, n = 28). Its relationship with clinicopathological parameters and plasma immune proteomics was examined.

RESULTS: Plasma LBP was elevated and associated with clinicopathological disease activity in therapy-naive CD and UC. In UC, but not CD, LBP related to disease extent, and strongly and independently correlated to endoscopic and histological disease activity, providing additional explanatory value beyond routinely used blood-based biomarkers and inflammatory mediators (including CRP and IL-6). LBP correlated with disease-specific immune profiles linked to severe disease (IL-17/neutrophil/IFN-γ-related profile in UC; IFN-γ-related profile in CD). LBP decreased after induction therapy with corticosteroids in UC, and after exclusive enteral nutrition or anti-TNF (but not corticosteroids) in CD. LBP remained associated with clinicopathological disease activity during follow-up, particularly in UC.

CONCLUSIONS: Plasma LBP relates to clinicopathological and immunological disease activity in IBD-especially UC-at diagnosis and during follow-up, and provides complementary information to routinely used blood-based biomarkers, arguing this simple, inexpensive parameter should be (re)considered for monitoring mucosal inflammation in UC.

Contemporary multicentre validation and comparative performance of Day 1 prognostic scores in acute severe ulcerative colitis

Subhaharan D, Mohsen W, Shahzad A, Patrick D, et al.

Ulcerative ColitisOriginal Research
Show abstract

BACKGROUND AND AIMS: Early identification of steroid non-response in acute severe ulcerative colitis (ASUC) is critical to guide timely escalation of therapy. Several Day 1 prognostic scores have been proposed, but their comparative performance in contemporary cohorts remains uncertain, with limited direct head-to-head validation in real-world populations. We aimed to validate and directly compare the performance of previously proposed Day 1 prognostic scores, including the Admission Model for Intensification of Therapy in Acute Severe Colitis (ADMIT-ASC), the Albumin-C reactive protein-Endoscopy (ACE) index, and the Risk of Rescue (ROR) score, in a contemporary multicenter ASUC cohort.

METHODS: We conducted a multicenter retrospective study of adults hospitalized with ASUC across three tertiary Australian centers between 2018 and 2023. The performance of ADMIT-ASC, ACE, and ROR scores for predicting steroid non-response, as defined by the Oxford criteria, was assessed using receiver operating characteristic analysis.

RESULTS: Among 238 patients, 134 (56.3%) met criteria for steroid non-response and required medical rescue therapy. All three scores demonstrated moderate discrimination, with AUROC highest for ROR (0.72), followed by ADMIT-ASC (0.68) and ACE (0.66) (P = .17). At published thresholds, ADMIT-ASC and ACE demonstrated high specificity but low sensitivity, whereas ROR provided greater sensitivity with lower specificity. In subgroup analyses, the discriminative performance of the Day 1 scores was broadly similar in biologic-naive and biologic-exposed patients.

CONCLUSION: Day 1 prognostic scores demonstrate moderate performance in contemporary ASUC, with differing trade-offs between sensitivity and specificity. These findings support the use of Day 1 risk scores as adjuncts to early clinical decision-making in ASUC.

Morganella morganii-derived histamine impairs intestinal barrier function via HRH4 and aggravates colitis

Yi Y, Feng R, Wu X, Zhu X, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseOriginal Research
Show abstract

BACKGROUND: Pro-inflammatory dietary patterns are associated with an increased risk of Crohn's disease (CD) and shifts in the gut microbiota. Our previous multi-omics analysis had identified the enrichment of Morganella morganii (M. morganii) in both patients with CD and individuals consuming pro-inflammatory diets. In this study, we aimed to investigate the role and potential mechanisms by which M. morganii contributes to intestinal inflammation.

METHODS: Using murine models of colitis, mouse colon organoids, and epithelial cell systems, combined with metabolomic profiling and pharmacological interventions, we investigated the effects of M. morganii and its secreted metabolites on tight junction integrity and intestinal permeability.

RESULTS: M. morganii aggravated colitis severity and induced a remarkable increase in intestinal permeability with disruption of tight junction architecture, including reduced occludin and zonula occludens-1 (ZO-1) expression and ultrastructural damage. These effects were predominantly mediated by the secreted low-molecular-weight metabolites rather than by bacterial invasion. Metabolomic analysis of the <3-kDa fraction of M. morganii culture supernatant identified histamine as a key bioactive molecule. Histamine recapitulated barrier disruption and colitis exacerbation in vivo. Pharmacological studies suggested that histamine-induced injury is mediated mainly through the histamine receptor H4 (HRH4) and that the inhibition of histamine synthesis significantly restored barrier integrity and alleviated colitis.

CONCLUSIONS: These findings suggest a previously unrecognized pathway in which the diet-responsive pathobiont M. morganii may impair epithelial barrier integrity through histamine-HRH4 signaling. Targeting histamine synthesis and HRH4 may offer a potential therapeutic avenue worth further investigation.

Phase 2 Trial of Icotrokinra for Patients with Ulcerative Colitis

Abreu MT, Siegmund B, Surace L, Erondu N, et al.

Ulcerative ColitisRCT
Show abstract

BACKGROUND: Monoclonal antibodies targeting the interleukin-23 pathway are effective in treating inflammatory bowel disease. However, these therapies must be administered intravenously or by subcutaneous injection.

METHODS: We conducted a phase 2b, double-blind, randomized trial of oral icotrokinra, a peptide that blocks the interleukin-23 receptor, in adults with moderately to severely active ulcerative colitis. Participants were assigned to receive icotrokinra at a once-daily dose of 400 mg, 200 mg, or 100 mg or placebo. The primary end point was clinical response at week 12. Secondary end points included clinical remission, remission of symptoms, and endoscopic improvement at week 12.

RESULTS: A total of 252 participants underwent randomization: 63 were assigned to the 400-mg group, 62 to the 200-mg group, 64 to the 100-mg group, and 63 to the placebo group. All the doses of icotrokinra were superior to placebo with respect to a clinical response at week 12, which occurred in 63% of the participants in the 400-mg group, 58% in the 200-mg group, and 55% in the 100-mg group, as compared with 27% in the placebo group (P<0.001 for all comparisons with placebo). The percentage of participants with clinical remission at week 12 was also significantly higher in the 400-mg group than in the placebo group (30% vs. 11%, P = 0.006), as were the percentage of participants with symptomatic remission (46% vs. 19%, P<0.001) and the percentage of participants with endoscopic improvement (37% vs. 14%, P = 0.002). Clinical remission at week 12 occurred in 24% of the participants in the 200-mg group (P = 0.054 for the comparison with placebo); in accordance with the fixed-sequence testing procedure to account for multiple comparisons, no further formal testing of secondary end points was conducted. Through week 28, the percentage of participants with adverse events was 60% in the 400-mg group, 66% in the 200-mg group, 66% in the 100-mg group, and 62% in the placebo group. For serious adverse events, the percentages were 2%, 5%, 0%, and 10%, respectively.

CONCLUSIONS: Among participants with moderately to severely active ulcerative colitis, a clinical response at week 12 was significantly more common with once-daily oral icotrokinra at each of three tested doses than with placebo.
