Maternal Obesity Is Associated With Adverse Pregnancy Outcomes in Inflammatory Bowel Disease: A Matched Electronic Health Record Cohort Study

Roberts C, Smith R, Odeh S, Kuk N, et al.

IBDOriginal Research
Show abstract

BACKGROUND: We sought to define the prevalence of obesity in the first trimester of pregnancy among women with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) and evaluate its association with adverse pregnancy outcomes.

METHODS: First-trimester body mass index (BMI) was assessed in pregnancies with and without IBD. Pregnancies reaching 24 weeks were propensity-score matched 1:6 by maternal age, BMI, smoking status and gravidity to assess whether IBD conferred additional pregnancy risk.

RESULTS: Overall, 22.6% (64/283; 95% CI 17.7%-27.5%) of pregnancies in women with IBD met ethnicity-adjusted criteria for obesity. Increasing obesity class was associated with stepwise increases in gestational diabetes, pre-eclampsia, venous thromboembolism (VTE), emergency caesarean section and high birthweight. Matching yielded 177 IBD pregnancies and 1062 controls. Obesity was independently associated with the composite adverse pregnancy outcome (OR 1.63; 95% CI 1.15-2.33; p = 0.007), whereas no statistically significant additional association was identified for IBD diagnosis alone (OR 0.74; 95% CI 0.53-1.03; p = 0.073). Among IBD pregnancies, aspirin was recommended in 39.3% (86/219). Low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH) was prescribed in 21.6% (19/88) of pregnancies classified post hoc as intermediate or high VTE risk.

CONCLUSION: More than one in five pregnancies in women with IBD were complicated by obesity. Obesity was strongly associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes, whereas this study did not identify a significant additional effect attributable to IBD diagnosis alone. Preventive prescribing with aspirin and LMWH represents an opportunity for improved antenatal care.

Endoscopic Balloon Dilation Versus Endoscopic Stricturotomy for Short Crohn's Disease Strictures: The BEST-CD Randomized Trial

Pal P, Pooja K, Naaz SM, Marri UK, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseOriginal Research
Show abstract

BACKGROUND AND AIMS: Endoscopic balloon dilation (EBD) is the standard therapy for Crohn's disease (CD) strictures but is limited by recurrence and reinterventions. Randomized comparative data on endoscopic stricturotomy (ES) vs EBD are lacking.

METHODS: In this randomized controlled trial, adults (18-65 years) with symptomatic CD and short (<3 cm) strictures accessible by standard endoscopy were randomly assigned (1:1) to EBD or ES. The primary outcome was clinical recurrence at 1 year. Secondary outcomes at 1 year included reintervention, surgery, and stricture-related emergency visits and hospitalisation; safety was assessed at 30 days. Time-to-event analyses were censored at 12 months.

RESULTS: Of 164 patients screened, 106 were randomised (EBD n=53; ES n=53). In the intention-to-treat population, clinical recurrence at 1 year was lower with ES than EBD (30.1% vs 54.7%; p=0·01), as were reintervention (24.5% vs 49.1%; p=0·01), emergency visits (18.9% vs 50.9%; p=0·001), and hospitalisations (15.1% vs 34%; p=0·02). Surgery did not differ significantly (1.9% vs 11.3%; p=0·11). In 12-month time-to-event analyses, EBD was associated with higher hazards of clinical recurrence (HR 2.27, 95% CI 1.23-4.18) and reintervention (HR 2.40, 95% CI 1.23-4.68), with consistent findings for emergency visits and hospitalisation. Adverse events were similar (13·2% vs 22.6%; p=0·30).

CONCLUSION: ES was associated with lower 1-year clinical recurrence and reintervention than EBD for short CD strictures. These findings support ES as a promising endoscopic strategy in expert centres and warrant validation in multicentre studies. (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05521867).

Comparison of risankizumab and upadacitinib treatment persistence according to disease location in patients with Crohn's disease: data from the CREdIT registry

Hradsky O, Duricova D, Lukas M, Nedbalova L, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseOriginal ResearchAnti-IL-23JAK inhibitor
Show abstract

IMPORTANCE: Comparative data on risankizumab and upadacitinib in Crohn's disease are limited.

OBJECTIVE: We aimed to compare the real-world effectiveness of the 2 therapies, measured by treatment persistence, and to assess whether it differed according to disease location.

DESIGN: Retrospective cohort study.

SETTING: Nationwide CREdIT prospective registry.

PARTICIPANTS: Pediatric and adult patients with Crohn's disease.

EXPOSURES: Initiation of risankizumab or upadacitinib.

MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES: The primary outcome was treatment persistence, defined as time from therapy initiation to drug discontinuation, with censoring at the last follow-up, and evaluated using weighted Cox models clustered by patient. Missing covariates were handled using multiple imputation, and treatment groups were balanced using inverse probability weighting (IPW) based on propensity scores.

RESULTS: A total of 560 treatment observations were included (264 risankizumab and 296 upadacitinib), most representing advanced lines of therapy. Following IPW, upadacitinib was associated with a higher risk of treatment discontinuation than risankizumab (Hazard ratio [HR] 1.86, 95% CI, 1.02-3.39). Among patients with ileal involvement (L1/L3), upadacitinib was associated with a higher discontinuation risk (HR 2.56, 95% CI, 1.49-4.40), whereas no difference was observed in patients with isolated colonic disease (L2; HR 0.66, 95% CI, 0.19-2.33). The interaction between upadacitinib therapy and isolated colonic involvement was significant (P = .049).

CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: In this nationwide real-world cohort of predominantly advanced treatment-experienced patients with Crohn's disease, risankizumab was associated with greater treatment persistence than upadacitinib. The superiority of risankizumab was observed mainly in patients with ileal involvement, and these findings suggest that disease location may be an important consideration when choosing between the 2 therapies.

Am J Gastroenterol Oct 28 2026

Preventing Clostridioides difficile Infection and Recurrence: Practical Strategies for Clinical Practice

Axelrad JE, Allegretti JR

IBDOriginal Research
Show abstract

Clostridioides difficile infection (CDI) is a major cause of morbidity, mortality, and healthcare utilization worldwide. Although advances in antimicrobial stewardship and infection prevention have reduced healthcare-associated disease in some settings, the incidence of community-associated CDI and the persistent burden of recurrent CDI (rCDI) continue to pose substantial clinical challenges. Because recurrent disease is fundamentally driven by persistent disruption of the intestinal microbiome and loss of colonization resistance, prevention has emerged as the central strategy for reducing long-term disease burden. This review summarizes contemporary evidence supporting prevention of both incident and rCDI, emphasizing practical approaches for gastroenterologists. Primary prevention centers on minimizing modifiable risk factors through antimicrobial stewardship, infection prevention and control, diagnostic stewardship to distinguish colonization from infection, and strategies to preserve the intestinal microbiome. For patients with CDI, we review evidence supporting extended antibiotic regimens, oral vancomycin prophylaxis during subsequent systemic antibiotic exposure, and microbiome restoration therapies, including conventional fecal microbiota transplantation and US Food and Drug Administration-approved microbiota-based therapies, which have transformed secondary prevention by restoring microbial diversity and colonization resistance. Special consideration is given to populations at particularly high risk for recurrence, including older adults, immunocompromised individuals, transplant recipients, and patients with inflammatory bowel disease. Finally, we discuss emerging preventive strategies, including precision risk stratification, microbiome- and metabolome-based biomarkers, bile acid modulation, and approaches targeting asymptomatic colonization. Together, these advances signal a paradigm shift from treating CDI episodes to implementing personalized preventive strategies that interrupt the cycle of recurrent disease and improve long-term patient outcomes.

Intravenous Versus Subcutaneous Induction with Guselkumab: Week 12 and Week 96 Efficacy in GALAXI 2 & 3 and GRAVITI

D'Haens GR, Afzali A, Danese S, Rubin DT, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseOriginal ResearchAnti-IL-23
Show abstract

BACKGROUND & AIMS: Guselkumab is approved for intravenous (IV) or subcutaneous (SC) induction in moderately to severely active Crohn's disease (CD). We compared outcomes and evaluated the onset of guselkumab efficacy with IV or SC induction and charted the long-term efficacy and safety of guselkumab SC maintenance following either route of induction.

METHODS: Treatment effects of IV and SC induction were compared via inverse probability weighting (IPW) analysis. Clinical, endoscopic, and inflammatory biomarker outcomes in participants randomized to placebo or guselkumab in GALAXI (IV induction) and GRAVITI (SC induction) were evaluated through Week 12, including analyses across predefined subgroups. Efficacy and safety outcomes through Week 96 were reported from the long-term extensions (LTEs) of GALAXI and GRAVITI.

RESULTS: IPW analysis found no statistical differences (all P > .05) between guselkumab IV and SC induction efficacy. In the per-trial comparisons with placebo, both IV and SC induction were statistically superior for Week 12 clinical response (Δ 34.5-42.8); similar efficacy was seen in Week 12 analyses of clinical remission rates (Δ 25.1-34.9), and abdominal pain (Δ 0.6-0.7) and stool frequency scores (Δ 1.2-2.0). Both routes of guselkumab induction provided rapid onset of efficacy, with clinical response and clinical remission observed by Week 4. IV and SC guselkumab induction regimens were similarly effective at Week 12 across prespecified subgroups of baseline disease location and clinical, endoscopic, and inflammatory biomarker disease activity. Adverse events during induction with SC and IV guselkumab were similar and comparable to placebo. Efficacy was maintained after 96 weeks of guselkumab therapy: long-term outcomes of clinical remission, endoscopic response, endoscopic remission, and deep remission were unaffected by the route of induction: 39.5-51.2% of guselkumab participants achieved deep remission at Week 96. Exposure-adjusted incidence rates of AEs, SAEs, and AEs leading to discontinuation through Week 96 were lower with guselkumab versus placebo and generally similar between GALAXI and GRAVITI.

CONCLUSIONS: IV and SC induction with guselkumab were equally effective across a wide range of patients with CD, including those with high baseline disease activity. Efficacy with guselkumab through Week 96 was unaffected by the route of induction. No new safety concerns were identified.

Hyperuricemia aggravates acute colitis through NLRP3-associated macrophage-lineage inflammatory responses

Ji J, Wang Y, Zhou T, Zhang J, et al.

IBDOriginal Research
Show abstract

BACKGROUND: Inflammatory bowel diseases (IBDs) are chronic, relapsing inflammatory disorders of the gastrointestinal tract with rising prevalence. Hyperuricemia has been associated with IBD, but whether elevated uric acid (UA) directly aggravates intestinal inflammation and how it affects immune responses remain unclear.

METHODS: We used hepatocyte-specific Glut9 knockout mice, allopurinol intervention, and acute UA administration in wild-type mice to assess dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced acute colitis. Lamina propria immune cells were profiled by expanded flow cytometry; phagocytic/macrophage-lineage and NLRP3 pathways were perturbed with clodronate liposomes, MCC950, and Nlrp3 siRNA. Bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs) and public human IBD mucosal transcriptomic datasets were analyzed.

RESULTS: Glut9 HKO mice developed higher serum UA and more severe DSS-induced colitis than controls, whereas allopurinol reduced serum UA and ameliorated disease. Acute UA administration during DSS exposure similarly worsened colitis in wild-type mice. Hyperuricemia expanded colonic monocyte/macrophage-lineage cells during DSS challenge, increased pro-inflammatory cells, and enhanced NLRP3 inflammasome-associated signaling. Clodronate-sensitive macrophage depletion and MCC950 attenuated disease severity. In BMDMs, soluble UA enhanced LPS-primed inflammatory activation and inflammasome-associated readouts, which were reduced by NLRP3 inhibition or knockdown. In human IBD mucosal transcriptomic datasets, purine metabolism scores positively correlated with inflammatory macrophage and NLRP3 inflammasome signatures.

CONCLUSIONS: Elevated UA aggravates DSS-induced acute colitis and amplifies inflammatory monocyte/macrophage-lineage responses associated with NLRP3 inflammasome signaling. These findings identify hyperuricemia as a metabolic modifier of acute intestinal injury and support further investigation of UA-linked innate immune activation in IBD.

TASTY AND HEALTHY DIET REDUCES FECAL CALPROTECTIN IN HIGH-RISK FIRST-DEGREE RELATIVES OF PATIENTS WITH CROHN'S DISEASE: PIONIR RANDOMIZED CROSSOVER TRIAL

Turner D, Kenigsberg S, Plotkin L, Aharoni-Frutkoff Y, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseOriginal Research
Show abstract

BACKGROUND: With the rising prevalence of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), preventing new-onset disease becomes a priority. The PIONIR (Preventing IBD ONset in Individuals at Risk) randomized controlled trial explored whether the Tasty&Healthy (T&H) diet reduces inflammatory markers in high-risk first-degree relatives (FDRs) of patients with Crohn's disease (CD).

METHODS: FDRs with persistently elevated fecal calprotectin (FC, >70μg/g) and, at most, nonspecific mucosal abnormalities (edema or ≤10 aphthous ulcers) on video-capsule endoscopy or ileocolonoscopy were randomized to T&H or habitual diet (HD) for 8 weeks, followed by crossover for additional 8 weeks. Treatment effect on the primary outcome, FC response, was analyzed using generalized estimating equations for the two-period, two-sequence crossover design, following the intention-to-treat principle.

RESULTS: Twenty-nine FDRs were randomized (n=14 T&H-first, n=15 HD-first). At week 8, median FC was lower with T&H (64μg/g [IQR 44-127]) vs. HD (112μg/g [74-227]; P=.022). FC decreased by ≥50% in 71% on T&H versus 27% on HD (RR=2.68 [95%CI 1.09-6.60]; P=.016). Across both crossover periods, estimated marginal mean FC declined from 185μg/g [95%CI 144-237] by 61% to 72μg/g [95%CI 57-91] with T&H, versus 38% to 114μg/g [95%CI 93-140] with HD (ratio 0.63 [95%CI 0.51-0.80], translating to 37% overall decrease with T&H; P<0.001). Six microbial species differed between diets after FDR correction. By week 8, 79% rated T&H acceptable and 83% had good or complete adherence.

CONCLUSION: These findings demonstrate the modifiability of low-grade intestinal inflammation in healthy high-risk FDRs with persistent FC elevation but without diagnostic endoscopic evidence of CD, supporting long-term dietary prevention trials (ClinicalTrials.gov #NCT05211518).

Baseline Clearance and Early Troughs Identify Candidates for Early Therapeutic Drug Monitoring During Infliximab Induction in Inflammatory Bowel Disease

Mohamedi JA, Hoffert Y, Geukens N, Rahier J, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseUlcerative ColitisOriginal ResearchAnti-TNF
Show abstract

BACKGROUND AND AIMS: The value of therapeutic drug monitoring during infliximab induction remains uncertain. We evaluated whether baseline infliximab clearance and early exposure metrics identify candidates for potential early proactive therapeutic drug monitoring during induction in inflammatory bowel disease.

METHODS: We analysed a multicenter Phase 4 study in which adults with Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis initiated standard infliximab therapy (5 mg/kg at Weeks 0, 2, 6, 14, 22 and 30) with predefined peak, intermediate and trough sampling. A population pharmacokinetic model characterized disposition and covariates. Logistic regression assessed exposure-response relationships. The primary outcome was week-30 combined remission (Crohn's disease: Harvey-Bradshaw Index ≤ 4; ulcerative colitis: Partial Mayo score ≤ 2; with faecal calprotectin < 100 μg/g and C-reactive protein ≤ 5 mg/L).

RESULTS: Seventy-five participants (33 female; median age 39 years, interquartile range 28-52) contributed pharmacokinetic data. Sixty-three were evaluable for exposure-response analyses. Twelve participants (19%) achieved combined remission (7/35 Crohn's disease; 5/28 ulcerative colitis). Lower serum albumin, higher body weight, and antibodies to infliximab increased clearance. In ulcerative colitis, baseline clearance and model-predicted week-6 trough concentration showed strong discrimination for combined remission (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve 0.88, 95% confidence interval 0.75-1.00; and 0.86, 0.69-1.00, respectively). Baseline clearance > 0.28 L/day and week-6 trough ≤ 21.6 mg/L identified high-risk subgroups with few remissions (0/18 and 1/20, respectively, p < 0.05). No robust predictors were identified in Crohn's disease.

CONCLUSIONS: A pragmatic, risk-stratified, model-informed approach centered on baseline clearance and early troughs may guide personalized infliximab induction in ulcerative colitis. External validation is warranted.

TRIAL REGISTRATION: EudraCT number: 2015-004618-10.

Fecal microbiota transplant in the treatment of inflammatory bowel diseases: new perspectives on delivery, mechanism, and clinical positioning

Javed A, Chrysostomou D, Marchesi JR, Roberts L, et al.

IBDOriginal Research
Show abstract

The gut microbiota, a complex and dynamic community of microorganisms, has emerged as a crucial determinant of human health and well-being in recent years. This microbial ecosystem plays a pivotal role in various physiological processes, including nutrient metabolism, immune system regulation, and intestinal homeostasis. Disruptions in the composition and function of the gut microbiota have been implicated in a wide range of diseases, such as Clostridioides difficile infection (CDI) and inflammatory bowel diseases (IBD). The elucidation of the fundamental relationship between human disease and the gut microbiome has positioned fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) as a transformative strategy in modern clinical practice. However, while FMT is increasingly explored for a diverse range of systemic diseases, its clinical implementation is relatively limited beyond the management of recurrent CDI. This review examines the evolving role of FMT within the therapeutic framework of chronic inflammation, focusing specifically on IBD. We compare the variable clinical outcomes in IBD with the highly successful model of recurrent CDI, highlighting mechanistic and clinical determinants of response in a different disease context. Additionally, we evaluate the progression of delivery strategies, from conventional fecal suspensions to standardized oral capsule formulations, and their implications for efficacy, safety, and scalability. Furthermore, we explore the diversifying clinical scenarios where FMT may be incorporated in the treatment paradigm of IBD, including primary treatment of active inflammation, prevention of relapse, and its place in combination with current immunosuppressive therapies.

Efficacy and Safety of Guselkumab in Patients with Crohn's Disease and Ulcerative Colitis: A Prospective Real-World Study

Shafrir A, Choi D, Capucci LC, Hannett A, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseUlcerative ColitisOriginal ResearchAnti-IL-23
Show abstract

BACKGROUND AND AIMS: Guselkumab (GUS), selective IL-23p19 inhibitor that also binds to CD64+ cells, is approved for the treatment of moderately-to-severely active Crohn's disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC). Real-world evidence of effectiveness and safety is needed.

METHODS: We prospectively enrolled adult patients with IBD initiating GUS at our tertiary center between 9/2024 and 1/2026. Patients were followed for up to 52 weeks. Clinical and biochemical data were collected at designated time points on patients' activity indices and adverse events (AEs). Clinical remission was defined as a Harvey-Bradshaw Index (HBI) <5 for CD or Simple Clinical Colitis Activity Index (SCCAI) ≤2 for UC.

RESULTS: We followed 234 patients who initiated GUS (142 CD, 92 UC). Week 12 clinical remission was achieved in 76.8% of patients with CD (HBI<5) and 80.7% of patients with UC (SCCAI≤2). Among patients with active disease at baseline, week 12 remission rates were 24/41 (58.5%) in CD and 35/48 (72.9%) in UC. Remission was maintained at month 6 in 88.4% in CD. In UC, remission rates at 6 months were maintained in 85.4% of patients. Normal CRP at week 12 was observed in 72.9% patients with CD and 90.0% in UC. Disease location, extent, or behavior were not associated with different response rates. AEs occurred in 15 patients (6.4%) and were mostly mild; 7 patients discontinued GUS due to AEs.

CONCLUSION: In this large real-world prospective experience of GUS for IBD, we describe high rates of remission and an overall safety profile that is similar to that seen in the pivotal trials.
