Prognostic factors and outcomes in upper gastrointestinal Crohn's disease: A scoping review

Elzaanoun R, Natt N, Lupas D, Yuan Y, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseOriginal Research
Show abstract

BACKGROUND: Upper gastrointestinal (UGI) involvement occurs in about 13% of patients with Crohn's disease (CD) and is variably defined across guidelines, which complicates prognosis. We mapped adult data on prognostic factors and outcomes in UGI‑CD and identified evidence gaps.

METHODS: A comprehensive search strategy was applied to MEDLINE, Embase, and Cochrane Central through November 26, 2025. We included studies reporting prognostic factors in patients with UGI-CD. We synthesized evidence across 3 domains: (1) patient and disease characteristics associated with UGI involvement at diagnosis, (2) the comparative prognosis of UGI-CD vs non-UGI disease, and (3) among patients with UGI-CD, factors associated with long-term adverse outcomes. Findings were synthesized narratively.

RESULTS: From 3297 screened records, 732 underwent full text assessment, and 43 primary studies were included. A total of 101 prognostic factor-outcome relationships were identified. In domain 1 (patient and disease characteristics associated with UGI involvement at diagnosis), younger age at onset was the most consistently reported factor associated with UGI involvement. In domain 2 (comparative prognosis vs non-UGI disease), studies evaluating pooled L4 disease frequently associated UGI involvement at diagnosis with higher surgical risk, incident stricture development, and adverse long-term outcomes. However, studies that distinguished anatomic subtypes suggested that risk is not uniform: jejunal/proximal small bowel disease generally followed a more aggressive course, whereas esophagogastroduodenal-limited disease appeared to have a comparatively more favorable prognosis. In domain 3 (among patients with UGI-CD, factors associated with long-term adverse outcomes), a higher Simple Endoscopic Score for Crohn's Disease was associated with subsequent surgery in a duodenal stricture cohort on univariable analysis; an independent prognostic effect was not established.

CONCLUSIONS: The available literature suggests that prognosis in UGI-CD is not uniform. Jejunal/proximal small bowel disease appears to follow a more aggressive course than esophagogastroduodenal-limited disease, although interpretation is limited by predominantly retrospective designs, heterogeneous definitions, and inconsistent adjustment for confounders.

Comparative Risk of Cancer with non-TNF Advanced Therapies Versus Anti-TNF Therapy in Inflammatory Bowel Disease

Ibrahim MK, Wanchaitanawong W, Tripathi I, Anvari S, et al.

IBDOriginal ResearchAnti-integrin
Show abstract

BACKGROUND: Safety is important when selecting advanced therapies for inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). Safety data for anti-interleukin therapy (anti-IL) or vedolizumab come from trials with limited follow-up, and direct comparisons with anti-tumor necrosis factors (anti-TNFs) for incident cancer are lacking. We evaluated cancer incidence in patients with IBD treated with anti-IL therapy or vedolizumab versus anti-TNFs.

METHODS: We conducted a retrospective cohort study of adults with IBD and no prior cancer who initiated anti-IL therapy, vedolizumab or anti-TNFs. Our primary outcome was incident cancer from 90 days after therapy initiation through discontinuation. We calculated incidence rates and performed Cox proportional hazards models.

RESULTS: We identified 4,019 advanced therapy starts: 995 anti-IL, 692 vedolizumab, and 2,332 anti-TNF starts, with mean therapy duration of 1096 days. In multivariable analysis, there was a trend towards a lower hazard of all cancers among anti-IL users compared to anti-TNF users (HR 0.47; 95% CI 0.21-1.04), which was significant on sensitivity analyses. There was no difference in hazard of skin cancers (HR 1.56; 95% CI 0.39-6.15); however, the hazard of non-skin cancer was significantly lower among anti-IL users compared to anti-TNF users (HR 0.28; 95% CI 0.09-0.81). When comparing vedolizumab to anti-TNFs, there was no significant difference in the hazard of all cancer (HR 0.52, 95% CI 0.24-1.12) and non-skin cancer (HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.29-1.39).

CONCLUSIONS: We found no evidence of an increased cancer risk with anti-IL therapy compared with anti-TNF therapy in patients with IBD. There was no difference in the cancer risk between vedolizumab and anti-TNF therapy.

Comparative effectiveness of infliximab and tofacitinib in advanced therapy-naive and -exposed patients with ulcerative colitis: a multicenter real-world study

Mizuta N, Kinoshita N, Kakimoto K, Hara A, et al.

Ulcerative ColitisOriginal ResearchAnti-TNFJAK inhibitor
Show abstract

BACKGROUND: and Aim: Infliximab (IFX) and tofacitinib (TOF) are advanced therapies for ulcerative colitis (UC), but real-world comparative data by prior advanced therapy (AT) exposure remain limited. We compared effectiveness by prior AT exposure.

METHODS: This multicenter retrospective cohort study included adults with UC who initiated IFX or TOF between June 2010 and May 2025. The primary analysis was restricted to May 2018 onward, when both were available in Japan. AT-naive and AT-exposed cohorts were analyzed separately using overlap weighting. The primary outcome was corticosteroid-free clinical remission at week 52.

RESULTS: Of 266 patients (137 IFX, 129 TOF), the primary analysis included 76 AT-naive (27 IFX, 49 TOF) and 120 AT-exposed (40 IFX, 80 TOF) patients. Week 14 remission did not differ significantly between treatments in either cohort. At week 52, remission numerically favored TOF in AT-naive patients (IFX, 43.9%; TOF, 64.5%; odds ratio [OR], 2.32; 95% CI, 0.84-6.44; P = .105), whereas remission was higher with TOF in the AT-exposed cohort (IFX, 19.0%; TOF, 38.8%; OR, 2.71; 95% CI, 1.08-6.80; nominal P = .034). Three-year treatment persistence did not differ significantly in either cohort but numerically favored TOF in AT-naive patients (IFX, 39.2%; TOF, 61.5%; hazard ratio, 0.64; 95% CI, 0.31-1.36). Multiple-imputation analyses yielded similar findings.

CONCLUSIONS: Week 52 remission numerically favored TOF in AT-naive patients and was higher with TOF in AT-exposed patients. Clinically relevant differences in 3-year persistence could not be ruled out, particularly in AT-naive patients. These findings are exploratory.

Epstein-Barr virus and inflammatory bowel disease: a systematic review of its clinical significance and potential pathogenic role

Estevinho MM, Said H, Nandy A, Snapper SB, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseOriginal ResearchAnti-TNF
Show abstract

BACKGROUND: Several studies report an association between Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) and inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). This review critically synthesizes current evidence on these association between EBV and IBD, its potential mechanisms, clinical implications, and research gaps.

METHODS: A systematic search identified studies investigating EBV in IBD using serological, molecular, and tissue-based approaches. Data on EBV prevalence, viral load, disease phenotype, treatment status, and clinical outcomes were extracted, and study quality was assessed. A narrative review examined proposed mechanisms that may potentially link EBV to intestinal inflammation.

RESULTS: From 7271 citations, 45 studies met inclusion criteria. EBV was more frequently detected in intestinal tissue from IBD patients than in controls, particularly in active or refractory disease, whereas findings for seroprevalence and blood-based markers were less consistent. Some longitudinal studies reported associations between prior EBV exposure and subsequent IBD, particularly Crohn's disease, but these observations do not establish a causal relationship. Proposed mechanisms involving EBV-infected B cells, molecular mimicry, and altered cytokine signaling provide biological plausibility but remain unproven in IBD. EBV latency programs and age at seroconversion may influence disease progression and reactivation risk, although data remain limited. Corticosteroids and therapy with infliximab plus azathioprine have been associated with higher EBV loads, especially in refractory disease, yet these findings may be confounded by disease severity and treatment exposure.

CONCLUSIONS: Current evidence supports an association between EBV and IBD, particularly in severe and refractory disease but does not establish-and equally does not exclude-a pathogenic role for EBV. Considerable methodological heterogeneity in study design and EBV detection methods and the observational nature of the available data limit causal interpretation. Prospective mechanistic studies are needed to determine whether EBV is a causal contributor, a disease modifier, or a marker of underlying immune dysregulation.

Cellular therapies and immune reprogramming in inflammatory bowel disease: lessons learned and the promise of CAR T-cell therapy

Parigi TL, Islam BN, Pizarro TT, Cominelli F

Ulcerative ColitisOriginal Research
Show abstract

Despite major therapeutic advances, inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) remains incurable, and many patients experience primary non-response, secondary loss of response, progressive bowel damage, or lifelong dependence on immunomodulatory therapy. Cellular immunotherapies offer the possibility of moving beyond chronic immune suppression toward durable immune reprogramming. Earlier approaches in IBD, including hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, mesenchymal stem cells, and regulatory T-cell-based therapies, have provided important proof-of-concept but have been limited by toxicity, inconsistent efficacy, local rather than systemic effects, or unresolved manufacturing and durability challenges. Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy represents the next-generation strategy that combines potent immune remodeling with antigen-specific targeting. Initially developed for hematological malignancies, CAR T cells have recently shown striking efficacy in refractory autoimmune diseases, particularly through CD19-mediated depletion of B-lineage cells and induction of drug-free remission. In ulcerative colitis, renewed interest in B cells has been driven by evidence of plasmablast expansion, IgG-skewed humoral responses, anti-integrin αvβ6 autoantibodies, and the limitations of conventional anti-CD20 depletion. The first reported case of multidrug-resistant ulcerative colitis treated with CD19 CAR T cells demonstrated rapid clinical, biochemical, endoscopic, and histological remission, providing proof-of-concept for deep B-cell targeting in IBD. However, major uncertainties remain, including long-term durability, relapse after B-cell reconstitution, safety, cost, feasibility, and the risk of CAR T-associated gastrointestinal toxicity. Prospective trials with mechanistic mucosal monitoring are now needed to define the therapeutic potential and appropriate positioning of CAR-based strategies in IBD.

Impact of in vitro fertilization on disease activity and pregnancy outcomes in women with inflammatory bowel disease

Khanna R, Garcia NM, Fenton C, Long M, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseUlcerative ColitisOriginal ResearchAnti-IL-12/23
Show abstract

BACKGROUND: Women with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) increasingly utilize assisted reproductive technologies (ARTs) like in vitro fertilization and oocyte cryopreservation. Data regarding ART associated IBD flare risk are limited. We evaluated the risk of disease exacerbation among women with IBD undergoing ART.

METHODS: This retrospective single-center study evaluated women with IBD undergoing ART. Electronic medical records were abstracted for demographics, IBD history, and fertility history. Disease activity was assessed via medication exposure, biomarkers, and healthcare utilization 6 months pre- and post-ART. The primary outcome was disease flare (therapy modification, steroid initiation, or IBD-related hospitalization) within 6 months of ART. Secondary outcomes included successful egg retrieval, biochemical pregnancy, and live birth. Demographics were analyzed at the patient level and outcome data at the ART encounter level.

RESULTS: We included 49 women (mean age 37.1 ± 4.01 years, body mass index 25.7 ± 4.72 kg/m2) who underwent 149 ART encounters. Most had Crohn's disease (63.3%) or ulcerative colitis (34.7%), with a mean disease duration of 13.4 ± 7.16 years. Pre-ART, therapies included ustekinumab (20.8%), anti-tumor necrosis factors (12.1%), and corticosteroids (6.7%), or no therapy (29.5%). In 149 ART encounters (86 oocyte retrievals and 63 embryo transfers), the overall disease flare rate was 3.4% (n = 5 of 149, representing 5 unique patients). Flares occurred after embryo transfer during established pregnancy (gestational week ≥8), with no flares occurring during or immediately following oocyte retrieval. Secondary endpoints showed low rates of new corticosteroid use (2.7%), IBD-related hospitalizations (0.7%), and medication dose escalation (0.7%). Among the 63 embryo transfers, biochemical pregnancy occurred in 63.5% (n = 40 of 63) and live births in 42.9% (n = 27 of 63).

CONCLUSIONS: Across all 149 ART encounters, patients with IBD demonstrated low flare risk and live birth rates comparable to the general population. Under multidisciplinary guidance, ART is a safe and viable option in IBD.

Interpretable machine learning applied to high-dimensional salivary proteomics in pediatric inflammatory bowel diseases

Rupp BT, Reyna J, Giunta A, Weaver T, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseUlcerative ColitisOriginal Research
Show abstract

BACKGROUND: Inflammatory bowel diseases (IBDs), including Crohn's disease (CD), ulcerative colitis (UC), and inflammatory bowel disease unclassified (IBD-U), are chronic inflammatory disorders of the gastrointestinal tract. Current methods for classification and longitudinal monitoring are invasive, expensive, and often delayed, limiting timely diagnosis and management. This proof-of-concept study reports the first application of high-dimensional salivary proteomics integrated with interpretable artificial intelligence/machine learning (ML) to define a minimal protein signature for pediatric IBD classification with the ultimate goal of informing therapeutic decision-making.

METHODS: Unstimulated saliva from pediatric CD, UC, and IBD-U patients was analyzed using Alamar Biosciences' NULISAseq 250 Protein Inflammation Panel. Logistic regression with recursive feature elimination identified a candidate minimal discriminative signature. Performance was further assessed using follow-up samples. SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) quantified patient-specific protein contributions and assessed biological similarity of IBD-U to CD and UC.

RESULTS: Differential abundance analysis between UC and CD revealed 53 significantly different proteins. ML identified a 14-protein signature comprising chemokines/cytokines (CCL1, IFNA1-IFNA13, IL12p70, IL34, TNFSF11/RANKL), receptors/ligands (CD40LG, ICOSLG, IL1R2, IL17RA), structural/tissue-remodeling proteins (CD93, GFAP, SPP1), and growth factors/immune modulators (GDF2, GZMA). The proposed model achieved 97.5% balanced accuracy in first-visit pilot samples and 80.2% with matched follow-up samples. SHAP revealed patient-specific drivers and suggested biological alignment of IBD-U cases toward CD- or UC-like profiles.

CONCLUSIONS: This first-in-field integration of salivary proteomics with interpretable artificial intelligence/ML demonstrates that accurate, noninvasive classification of pediatric IBD is possible using minimal biomarker sets. This approach establishes a scalable framework for future longitudinal monitoring and supports earlier and more precise therapeutic interventions.

Microbial and antimicrobial resistance signatures differentiate healthy and inflamed ileal pouches in patients with ulcerative colitis

Dall SM, Møllekær AH, Kousgaard SJ, Jakobsen SS, et al.

Ulcerative ColitisPouchitisOriginal Research
Show abstract

BACKGROUND AND AIMS: Fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) from healthy donors with a normally functioning colon has limited clinical effect in the treatment of chronic pouchitis. However, a normally functioning ileal pouch-anal anastomosis (IPAA) physiology differs from individuals with a healthy intact colon. We aimed to investigate the microbial characteristics of patients with a normally functioning IPAA (HealthyPouch) compared with chronic pouchitis (SickPouch), ulcerative colitis (UC), and a healthy intact colon microbiome (NormalGut). Furthermore, we investigated how repeated antibiotic use influences antimicrobial resistance on a bacterial genome level.

METHODS: Fecal samples from 190 participants were collected; HealthyPouch (n = 38), SickPouch (n = 37), UC (n = 23), and NormalGut (n = 92) and sequenced with Illumina and taxonomically profiled. Samples from HealthyPouch, SickPouch, and NormalGut were also long-read sequenced using Oxford Nanopore Technologies for reconstruction of 12 282 metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs). DNA methylation motifs were used for plasmid-host association.

RESULTS: The HealthyPouch microbiome is distinct from both the UC, NormalGut, and SickPouch microbiome. Escherichia coli MAGs in the SickPouch (n = 21) microbiome had significantly higher co-occurring ciprofloxacin resistance mutations in the gyrA and parC genes, compared to both HealthyPouch (n = 26; BH adjusted P < .05) and NormalGut (n = 40; BH adjusted P < .05). Based on DNA methylation pattern, three plasmids were associated with E. coli carrying extended-spectrum beta lactamase genes.

CONCLUSIONS: HealthyPouch microbiota differs from NormalGut microbiota, and could therefore be more appropriate for FMT treatment of chronic pouchitis. The association between E. coli and resistance gene-carrying plasmids underscores the importance of integrating plasmid analysis into metagenome-based approaches for personalized antibiotic treatment and resistance monitoring.

B cell/adipocyte crosstalk amplifies MCP-1 secretion, M2-like macrophage accumulation, and polarization in gut-associated, inflamed adipose tissue

Goess MC, Heinze JM, Hofmann J, Buck A, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseOriginal Research
Show abstract

BACKGROUND AND AIMS: During Crohn's disease (CD), hyperplasia of the mesenteric white adipose tissue (WAT), called creeping fat (CrF), is associated with the accumulation of immune cells. Here we investigated the impact of B cell/adipocyte crosstalk on immune cell recruitment and inflammation within inflammatory bowel disease-related adipose tissue.

METHODS: We used flow cytometry and in vitro 3D culture systems of biopsies from CrF of CD patients as well as a mouse model of intestinal inflammation, in vitro co-cultures, mass spectrometry, and single-cell RNA sequencing to evaluate the impact of B cell/adipocyte interplay on monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (MCP-1) production and macrophage polarization.

RESULTS: Employing primary human and mouse 3D adipocyte cultures, we observed that adipocytes from CD patients displayed a persistent inflammatory phenotype even after prolonged time in culture, a cytokine secretion profile also recapitulated via lipopolysaccharide stimulation. Analyzing CrF of CD patients as well as WAT of a mouse model of intestinal inflammation, we found an accumulation of B cells. B cell/adipocyte co-cultures led to elevated secretion of MCP-1 by primary adipocytes, facilitating macrophage migration. Consequently, co-transfer of B cells with T cells increased T cell activation and M2-like macrophage accumulation in inflamed mouse WAT. Mass spectrometry and single-cell RNA sequencing analyses suggested that the B cell/adipocyte crosstalk establishes a microenvironment that may promote M2-like macrophage polarization.

CONCLUSION: B cell/adipocyte interactions in CD-associated inflamed adipose tissue increase MCP-1 and lactate, promoting recruitment and M2-like polarization of macrophages, likely amplifying local WAT inflammation.

Value of non-contrast magnetic resonance imaging for diagnosis of perianal fistulas in Crohn's disease

Lewis S, Ahluwalia A, Landis D, Altinmakas E, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseOriginal Research
Show abstract

PURPOSE: To compare non-contrast (NC)-MRI and contrast-enhanced (CE)-T1-weighted imaging (T1-WI) for the detection and characterization of perianal fistulizing Crohn's disease (PFCD).

METHODS: This Institutional Review Board-approved retrospective study included patients with Crohn's disease (CD) who underwent pelvic MRI (T2-WI, diffusion-weighted imaging [DWI], pre and post-contrast T1-WI) from 10/2021 to 12/2022. Two independent observers assessed presence and severity of perianal disease (St. James Criteria) and abscesses (presence/size) in 2 separate sessions: NC-MRI (T2-WI, DWI, pre-contrast-T1-WI) and CE-MRI (NC-MRI + post-contrast-T1-WI) separated by 6 weeks. Agreement between modalities was evaluated using weighted Cohen's kappa. Performance of NC-MRI was assessed against CE-MRI as the reference standard.

RESULTS: One hundred and forty-five patients (M/F 92/53, mean age 36.2 ± 15.1 years) were included. On NC-MRI, an index fistula was detected in 115/145 patients (grade 1 [n = 55]/grade 2 [n = 15]/grade 3 [n = 16]/grade 4 [n = 33]/grade 5 [n = 6]). On CE-MRI, an index fistula was detected in 117/145 patients (grade 1 [n = 36]/grade 2 [n = 39]/grade 3 [n = 8]/grade 4 [n = 29]/grade 5 [n = 5]). An abscess was detected on NC-MRI in 53 patients (37%) (mean size = 3.3 cm) and on CE-MRI in 63 patients (43%) (mean size = 3.0 cm). There was fair-to-substantial agreement between NC-MRI and CE-MRI (range, 0.46 [presence of secondary tracts] to 0.87 [presence of index fistula]), except for secondary fistula length (no agreement, kappa = 0.008). Compared to CE-MRI, NC-MRI demonstrated a sensitivity/specificity/PPV/NPV/accuracy of 0.96/0.93/0.98/0.84/0.95 for index fistula detection and 0.77/0.95/0.92/0/84/0.87 for abscess detection.

CONCLUSIONS: NC-MRI has high agreement with CE-MRI for index perianal fistula and abscess detection with clinical feasibility in CD patients. CE-MRI had added value for assessment of complex PFCD, including secondary fistulas and tracts.

CLINICAL RELEVANCE: NC-MRI had high agreement with CE-MRI for detecting perianal fistulas and substantial agreement for grading. CE-MRI has added value in diagnosing complex perianal disease. NC-MRI of the pelvis is a reasonable alternative for perianal fistula detection and grading for patients who cannot receive contrast or for potentially abbreviated exams.

ECCO Topical Review: Inflammatory Bowel Disease-Like Patterns of Drug Colitis with Practical Guidance and a Focus on Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor-Induced Enterocolitis

Verstockt B, Dragoni G, van Schaik F, Nunes PB, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseMicroscopic ColitisReviewAnti-TNFAnti-integrin
Show abstract

BACKGROUND: Drug-induced enterocolitis (DIC) consists of a heterogeneous group of gastrointestinal inflammatory disorders with clinical, endoscopic, and histological features that often overlap with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). Given the increasing recognition of immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-induced enterocolitis and the lack of standardized management algorithms, the European Crohn's and Colitis Organisation (ECCO) convened a multidisciplinary panel to review DIC, with a particular focus on ICI-induced disease.

METHODS: Gastroenterologists, histopathologists, and oncologists systematically reviewed the literature using Medline, PubMed, EMBASE, and Scopus. Evidence was synthesized into ECCO Current Practice Positions addressing diagnostic principles, histopathological patterns, therapeutic strategies, and ICI-specific considerations. Statements were refined and approved through structured consensus.

RESULTS: Twenty-three practice positions were agreed upon. DIC may present with diverse histological patterns, including IBD-like/chronic enterocolitis, acute enterocolitis, microscopic colitis, ischemic-type injury, and mixed features, none of which are pathognomonic. Close clinicopathological collaboration is therefore essential. In suspected ICI-induced enterocolitis, assessment should include Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events-based grading, stool testing for pathogens, fecal calprotectin, endoscopy with biopsies, and imaging when indicated. Corticosteroids remain first-line therapy, while mild or microscopic disease may respond to budesonide or beclomethasone. Corticosteroid-refractory or corticosteroid-dependent disease should prompt early escalation to infliximab or vedolizumab. Surgery is reserved for refractory or complicated cases. Rechallenge with ICIs may be considered following multidisciplinary evaluation.

CONCLUSION: DIC represents a diagnostically complex spectrum of disorders that require integrated multidisciplinary evaluation. These ECCO Current Practice Positions provide a practical framework for diagnostic work-up and treatment, particularly for ICI-induced enterocolitis.

Crohn's DiseaseOriginal ResearchAnti-integrinAnti-IL-12/23
Show abstract

BACKGROUND & AIMS: We conducted a retrospective cohort study comparing the risk of perianal penetrating complications with advanced therapies (AT) in patients with non-penetrating Crohn's disease (CD).

METHODS: Using an administrative claims database (OptumLabs Data Warehouse), we identified adult patients with CD who initiated treatment with tumor necrosis factor (TNF) antagonists, vedolizumab, ustekinumab, IL-23p19 antagonists or Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors between 2016 and 2022. Patients with any CD-related penetrating complication, abdominal or perianal, during the baseline 1 year and up to 3 months post AT initiation were excluded. RESULTS: We identified 10 482 patients with non-penetrating CD who initiated TNF antagonists (n = 4463), vedolizumab (n = 2372), ustekinumab (n = 3027), IL-23p19 antagonists (n = 480) and JAK inhibitors (n = 140), of whom 360 (3.4%) developed perianal penetrating complications over a mean follow-up of 18.511.2 months. CONCLUSIONS: Among patients with non-penetrating CD starting an AT, TNF antagonists and ustekinumab were associated with a lower risk of developing perianal penetrating complications compared to vedolizumab.

Treatment patterns and determinants of persistence with advanced therapies in adults with newly diagnosed Crohn's disease: an interim analysis of an inception cohort registry study (iCREST-CD)

Shinzaki S, Matsuoka K, Fujii T, Okamoto R, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseOriginal ResearchAnti-TNFAnti-integrinAnti-IL-12/23
Show abstract

BACKGROUND AND AIMS: Multiple advanced therapies are available for the treatment of Crohn's disease (CD). Treatment patterns in newly diagnosed patients and the factors associated with persistence remain unclear. We present interim results of the iCREST-CD registry describing treatment patterns and persistence with advanced therapies.

METHODS: Patients aged ≥16 years with newly diagnosed CD and who had no prior exposure to advanced therapies were enrolled. Persistence of advanced therapies was estimated using Kaplan-Meier analyses, and associated factors were identified using multivariable Cox proportional hazards models. Persistence was also compared between patients who initiated therapy early or later after diagnosis.

RESULTS: Of 672 patients (68.2% male; mean age 29.4 ± 13.1 years), 498 (74.1%) initiated advanced therapy: infliximab (IFX; 121), adalimumab (ADA; 244), ustekinumab (UST; 107), or vedolizumab (VDZ; 26). Advanced therapy was initiated within 1 year of diagnosis in 404 (60.1%). The 36-month treatment persistence rates for IFX, ADA, UST, and VDZ were 77.5%, 75.7%, 79.8%, and 49.0%, respectively. After propensity score adjustment using inverse probability of treatment weighting, the early initiation was associated with greater persistence than delayed initiation, although the difference did not reach statistical significance (hazard ratio, 0.61; 95% confidence interval, 0.35-1.09; P = .067).

CONCLUSIONS: A high prevalence of early advanced therapy use and a high level of persistence with first-line advanced therapy were identified in this national cohort. Early initiation was associated with greater treatment persistence than delayed initiation, suggesting the potential clinical relevance of earlier advanced therapy use in routine practice. These findings require confirmation in other prospective studies aiming to evaluate early intervention strategies.

REGISTRATION: The study was registered with the University Hospital Medical Information Network Clinical Trials Registry (umin.ac.jp/ctr Identifier: UMIN000032237) prior to initiation.

Peripheral T- and B-cell immune signatures associated with response to vedolizumab in ulcerative colitis

Horitani S, Horitani A, Tyler CJ, Naganuma M, et al.

Ulcerative ColitisOriginal ResearchAnti-integrin
Show abstract

BACKGROUND AND AIMS: Vedolizumab (VDZ) blocks integrin α4β7-MAdCAM-1 interactions, limiting leukocyte entry into the gut, yet peripheral-blood immune correlates of clinical response in ulcerative colitis (UC) remain unidentified.

METHODS: Peripheral blood mononuclear cells from healthy controls (HCs) and patients with UC, stratified by VDZ exposure and clinical response, were analyzed using mass cytometry (cytometry by time-of-flight). A 38-marker panel enriched for integrins, chemokine receptors, and activation markers was applied. Major immune populations were interrogated using lineage-specific opt-SNE and FlowSOM clustering.

RESULTS: The dataset included 26 HCs, 30 VDZ-naive UC (Nancy index ≥ 2), 8 VDZ-treated non-responders (Nancy index ≥ 2), and 8 VDZ-treated responders (Nancy index <2). No significant differences were detected in myeloid, NK-cell, or TCRγδ+ T-cell compartments. Integrin α4β7+ cells were detected across multiple immune lineages and were enriched in the B-cell compartment. Between-group differences were observed in the CD4+ T-cell and B-cell compartments. VDZ responders showed enrichment of an α4β7+ effector memory CD4+ T-cell subset compared with VDZ-naive UC. In parallel, responders exhibited reduced frequencies of class-switched plasmablast and activated B-cell populations relative to VDZ-naive UC. Integrin-pairing analysis demonstrated that these B-cell populations contained both α4β7+ and α4β1+ cells. In contrast, VDZ non-responders showed enrichment of an α4β7+ atypical-like IgA+ memory B-cell subset relative to responders, VDZ-naive UC, and HCs.

CONCLUSIONS: Clinical response and non-response to VDZ in UC are associated with distinct circulating T- and B-cell immune phenotypes.
