Efficacy and Safety of Guselkumab in Patients with Crohn's Disease and Ulcerative Colitis: A Prospective Real-World Study

Shafrir A, Choi D, Capucci LC, Hannett A, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseUlcerative ColitisOriginal ResearchAnti-IL-23
Show abstract

BACKGROUND AND AIMS: Guselkumab (GUS), selective IL-23p19 inhibitor that also binds to CD64+ cells, is approved for the treatment of moderately-to-severely active Crohn's disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC). Real-world evidence of effectiveness and safety is needed.

METHODS: We prospectively enrolled adult patients with IBD initiating GUS at our tertiary center between 9/2024 and 1/2026. Patients were followed for up to 52 weeks. Clinical and biochemical data were collected at designated time points on patients' activity indices and adverse events (AEs). Clinical remission was defined as a Harvey-Bradshaw Index (HBI) <5 for CD or Simple Clinical Colitis Activity Index (SCCAI) ≤2 for UC.

RESULTS: We followed 234 patients who initiated GUS (142 CD, 92 UC). Week 12 clinical remission was achieved in 76.8% of patients with CD (HBI<5) and 80.7% of patients with UC (SCCAI≤2). Among patients with active disease at baseline, week 12 remission rates were 24/41 (58.5%) in CD and 35/48 (72.9%) in UC. Remission was maintained at month 6 in 88.4% in CD. In UC, remission rates at 6 months were maintained in 85.4% of patients. Normal CRP at week 12 was observed in 72.9% patients with CD and 90.0% in UC. Disease location, extent, or behavior were not associated with different response rates. AEs occurred in 15 patients (6.4%) and were mostly mild; 7 patients discontinued GUS due to AEs.

CONCLUSION: In this large real-world prospective experience of GUS for IBD, we describe high rates of remission and an overall safety profile that is similar to that seen in the pivotal trials.

Gastroenterology Oct 21 2026

Microbiota-Taurine Pathway Links Dietary Fiber to Mucosal Healing

Odakura R, Seki N, Nomura K, Inoue Y, et al.

Ulcerative ColitisOriginal Research
Show abstract

BACKGROUND AND AIMS: Dietary fibers modulate host-microbiota interactions. Whether fiber promotes epithelial repair through microbiota-dependent mechanisms remains unclear. We determined how alginate influences colitis recovery and whether the pathway is relevant to ulcerative colitis (UC).

METHODS: We screened fibers in dextran sodium sulfate (DSS)-induced murine colitis and assessed survival, inflammation, barrier function, and epithelial regeneration. Microbiota dependence was evaluated using antibiotics and bacterial supplementation. Metabolites were profiled using capillary electrophoresis-time-of-flight mass spectrometry (CE-TOFMS), and epithelial programs were analyzed using RNA-seq and colonic organoid injury assays. Slc6a6-/- and Cyp2c55-/- mice were used to determine mechanistic requirements. Translational relevance was examined in a randomized, placebo-controlled trial of UC patients receiving antibiotic-primed fecal microbiota transplantation (A-FMT) followed by alginate or placebo. Clinical outcomes, histology, fecal taurine, and colonic CYP2C18 expression were assessed.

RESULTS: Alginate improved DSS colitis recovery, reducing inflammation, restoring barrier integrity, and enhancing crypt proliferation. Alginate enriched taurine-releasing bacteria, including Bacteroides acidifaciens and Ligilactobacillus murinus, and increased intestinal taurine; antibiotic disruption abrogated these effects. Taurine was required for benefit, as alginate failed in Slc6a6-/- mice. Alginate and taurine induced epithelial Cyp2c55, and protection was lost in Cyp2c55-/- mice and organoids. In the clinical trial, the primary endpoint was not met. However, alginate supplementation was associated with biological changes, including fecal taurine levels and colonic CYP2C18 expression, consistent with preclinical mechanisms.

CONCLUSION: Alginate engages a fiber-microbiota-taurine-epithelial signaling pathway that promotes recovery in preclinical models. The clinical trial did not meet its primary endpoint; however, the biological changes warrant further investigation of alginate as a microbiota-targeted intervention.

CLINICAL TRIAL REGISTRY: jRCTs031200103 (https://jrct.mhlw.go.jp/), registered September 1, 2020: UMIN000041968 (https://www.umin.ac.jp/ctr/index.htm), October 1, 2020.

Endoscopic and histologic healing distribution and persistent rectosigmoiditis in moderate-to-severe ulcerative colitis

Ma C, Hanzel J, Sedano R, Laroux FS, et al.

Ulcerative ColitisOriginal ResearchAnti-TNF
Show abstract

BACKGROUND: Mucosal healing in ulcerative colitis (UC) is believed to follow a proximal-to-distal pattern. We evaluated colonic healing distribution in patients with moderate-to-severe UC receiving adalimumab.

METHODS: Patients from the SERENE UC (Study of a Novel Approach to Induction and Maintenance Dosing with Adalimumab in Patients with Moderate to Severe Ulcerative Colitis) trial with full colonoscopies and segmental biopsies at screening, weeks 8, and 52 were included for endoscopic and histologic analyses if baseline Mayo endoscopic subscore (MES) or Robarts Histopathology Index (RHI) score, respectively, was >0 in the ascending, transverse, and/or descending colon. Data were analyzed using a mixed model for repeated measures to account for clustering of segments within a patient.

RESULTS: Endoscopic and histologic analyses comprised 76 and 79 patients, respectively. Score reductions were significantly greater vs the rectum in the ascending (week 8: MES, mean -0.78 [95% confidence interval (CI), -1.03 to -0.52]; RHI, mean -4.70 [95% CI, -6.92 to -2.49]; week 52: MES, mean -0.63 [95% CI, -0.91 to -0.35]; RHI, mean -2.39 [95% CI, -4.64 to -0.15]), transverse (week 8: MES, mean -0.69 [95% CI, -0.94 to -0.43]; RHI, mean -3.57 [95% CI, -5.62 to -1.51]; week 52: MES, mean -0.64 [95% CI, -0.89 to -0.38]; RHI, mean -2.88 [95% CI, -4.99 to -0.77]), and descending colon (week 8: MES, mean -0.40 [95% CI, -0.67 to -0.13]; RHI, mean -2.55 [95% CI, -4.94 to -0.16]; week 52: MES, mean -0.52 [95% CI, -0.70 to -0.34]; RHI, mean -3.47 [95% CI, -5.37 to -1.56]). Endoscopic improvement/remission and histologic remission were significantly more likely in the ascending and transverse colon than the rectum.

CONCLUSIONS: Our findings demonstrate that endoscopic and histologic healing is more challenging in the rectum compared with proximal colonic segments.

Aliment Pharmacol Ther Oct 21 2026

Meta-Analysis: Safety and Effectiveness of Advanced Therapies in Inflammatory Bowel Disease Patients With Concomitant Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis

Elzubeir A, Kumar A, McArdle A, Holden E, et al.

IBDReviewAnti-TNFAnti-integrinJAK inhibitor
Show abstract

BACKGROUND: Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) associated with primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC) is a distinct phenotype (PSC-IBD). Data on the safety and effectiveness of advanced IBD therapies on IBD outcomes in PSC-IBD remain limited.

METHODS: A systematic review and meta-analysis assessed the safety and effectiveness of advanced therapies in adults with PSC-IBD. MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane databases and Web of Science were searched to July 2026. The primary outcome was IBD clinical remission. Secondary outcomes included clinical response, corticosteroid-free remission, mucosal healing, adverse event-related treatment discontinuation and colorectal neoplasia. A random-effects model was used for pooled estimates.

RESULTS: Five retrospective case-series (n = 394) evaluating infliximab, adalimumab, vedolizumab and tofacitinib were included (median follow-up of 18.4 months). Pooled clinical remission rates were 24.7% (95% CI: 17.2%-33.0%) at 3 months and 28.6% (95% CI: 16.3%-42.6%) at 6-12 months. Corticosteroid-free remission was achieved in 45.4% (95% CI: 33.1%-57.9%) and mucosal healing in 25.6% (95% CI: 13.6%-39.5%). Tofacitinib was associated with a clinical response rate of 59.3% (95% CI: 40.0%-77.2%) and low adverse event rate, in a single available study. Adverse event-related discontinuation occurred in 9.1% (95% CI: 4.4%-15.1%), while 31.8% (95% CI: 25.5%-38.5%) experienced primary non-response or secondary loss of response. The pooled proportion of colorectal neoplasia was 10.5% (12/112; 95% CI: 5.3%-17.1%). Five patients developed colorectal cancer (5/112; 4.3%) and two (2/177; 0.8%) cholangiocarcinoma.

CONCLUSION: Advanced IBD therapies demonstrate modest but encouraging effectiveness and an acceptable safety profile in PSC-IBD; however, the available evidence is limited by retrospective study designs, small sample sizes and substantial clinical and methodological heterogeneity. Prospective controlled studies using standardised IBD outcomes are needed to inform optimal therapeutic strategies in this high-risk population.

Large-scale proteomics identifies a pretreatment Crohn's disease core proteome and a dynamic signature for anti-TNF response

Karns R, Hyams JS, Boyle BM, Noe JD, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseOriginal ResearchAnti-TNF
Show abstract

BACKGROUND: With modest rates of endoscopic remission to anti-TNF biologics in children with moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease (CD), there is a critical need to develop noninvasive biomarkers associated with anti-TNF response. We performed a comprehensive proteomic evaluation to identify peripheral-blood candidate biomarkers for anti-TNF response.

METHODS: The ENvISION study enrolled patients (2-19 years old) with CD prior to starting infliximab or adalimumab with outcomes prospectively monitored at Month 3 (M3) and Month 12 (M12). The SomaScan® (Somalogic, Boulder, CO, 7322 analytes) was performed prior to anti-TNF treatment (baseline) at M3 and M12. Endoscopic remission was assessed at M12 (simple endoscopic score-CD < 3).

RESULTS: Eighty patients with CD were enrolled with the CD Core Proteome developed from 61 prednisone-naive CD and 30 healthy controls. We identified 2999 differentially abundant proteins, 1403 with lower abundance and 1596 with higher abundance (FDR < 0.05). The CD Core Proteome was then compared across the three timepoints (baseline, M3 and M12) and we found that at both M3 and M12, the Core Proteome dysregulation retains strong identity with the baseline protein signal (β = .7, P < .001; β = .63, P < .001, respectively). Although the proteome exhibits change over time, it does not revert to a control-like state; rather, it transitions to a distinct "treated CD" proteome. In an exploratory investigation of the change in (delta) protein abundance from baseline to M3, we identified 132 proteins nominally associated with M12 endoscopic remission (P < .01). Using elastic net regression, we identified a three-protein panel (IRF1, NEUROG1, and APOA5) associated with M12 endoscopic remission (AUROC 0.94, 95% CI 0.88-1.0).

CONCLUSIONS: In an exploratory investigation, we identified a CD Core Proteome and three-protein panel whose early changes were associated with M12 endoscopic remission, supporting their use as early pharmacodynamic response markers following external validation.

Updated treatment targets in inflammatory bowel disease (STRIDE-III): an initiative of the International Organization for the Study of IBD

Turner D, Ricciuto A, Yuval Bar-Asher S, Vuyyuru SK, et al.

Crohn's DiseaseUlcerative ColitisReview
Show abstract

Since Selecting Therapeutic Targets in Inflammatory Bowel Disease-II (STRIDE-II), new evidence has become available across inflammatory and patient-centred outcomes. We aimed to update the STRIDE recommendations for Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis. We searched MEDLINE, Embase, and Cochrane CENTRAL for studies published after the STRIDE-II search period. Findings were integrated with surveys of International Organization for the Study of IBD (IOIBD) members and paediatric gastroenterologists from the Porto group of the European Society for Paediatric Gastroenterology, Hepatology and Nutrition. Statements were subsequently voted on using a modified Delphi approach; consensus was defined as ≥75% of participants rating a statement 7-10 on a 10-point scale. Of 13 071 records identified in the systematic review, 1018 were included. Surveys were completed by 60 of 79 IOIBD gastroenterologists and 23 of 30 paediatric gastroenterologists in the Porto group. STRIDE-III defines three conceptually distinct categories of treatment targets. Actionable targets include clinical response and remission, which reflect patient priorities, and objective inflammatory targets (C-reactive protein normalisation, faecal calprotectin within target range, and endoscopic remission), supported by interventional evidence demonstrating benefits of target-guided treatment modification. Adjunctive targets (ie, urgency, histological remission in ulcerative colitis, transmural remission in Crohn's disease, and growth) are supported by studies showing association with favourable outcomes and guide treatment optimisation and tighter monitoring but do not necessarily require a change in therapeutic class. Patient-centred targets (ie, disability, fatigue, and quality of life) directly reflect patients' well-being and are impaired in a substantial proportion of patients with inflammatory bowel disease; impairment should prompt assessment of inflammation and alternative causes and management through shared decision-making. Targets are further classified as immediate, short-term, intermediate, and long-term. STRIDE-III also defines treatment-specific times to target achievement, new faecal calprotectin thresholds, and adaptations for resource-limited settings. STRIDE-III distinguishes targets that should prompt treatment change from those that primarily guide optimisation and monitoring, while establishing a separate patient-centred pathway based on shared decision-making. This framework supports consistent, evidence-based treat-to-target care.
